The Scale of Impact

A Polish Educational Staple

With over 100,000 users, Wolne Lektury serves as the primary gateway to the Polish literary canon — for a lot of students, it's the only way they access these texts.

A digital library stuck in the past

Despite its essential role, the app was stuck in a previous era of mobile design. I started this project as a reader like myself, frustrated by an interface that felt more like a technical database than a library.

I wanted to see if others felt the same. Through my research, I found that the "aging" interface wasn't just a visual issue—it was creating a functional barrier within education.

01 / THE PROBLEM

The Friction of Literacy

Classic literature is inherently demanding. When the digital medium adds "chaotic menus" and "confusing filters" to an already difficult task, it becomes another barrier before someone even reaches the text. I found that even with a strong need for accessible classics, the current friction was discouraging users from the books themselves.

Fig 01. Analyzing the navigation debt in the original application.

02 / RESEARCH FINDINGS

Design as a Trust Signal

I evaluated the interface myself as a regular reader, then tested it further with a small group of friends who read regularly — asking them to find a book, save it to continue reading later, and explain what becoming a "Patron" meant. The reactions were consistent: the interface felt cluttered, and features that should have been simple were easy to miss entirely.

I.

Color Overload

Too many competing colors distracted from the reading experience.

II.

Legibility

Titles and typography were hard to distinguish at a glance, especially across similar covers.

III.

Feature Fog

Most people I tested with couldn't explain what the "Patron" feature actually did.

USER JOURNEY

Where the Friction Actually Was

I asked testers to find a book and then resume reading it from the main screen. The friction wasn't really about the number of steps — it was hesitation: similar-looking covers and titles made it hard to tell books apart, and it wasn't always obvious which tap would get them back to where they'd left off.

03 / USER PERSONA

User Persona: The Time-Poor Student

While the library is open to everyone, Wolne Lektury targets students as their core audience. The primary pain points are felt most by users like Maciek. For him, the app isn't just a library; it's a tool that needs to compete with a busy work-study schedule.

Maciek, 17

Student Unmotivated Busy

"I have 15 minutes to find a reference—if I have to dig through the catalogue again to find my book, I'll get annoyed."

Primary Need

A streamlined reading dashboard that prioritizes Progress Tracking and Instant Library Access to maximize limited study windows.

04 / PROTOTYPING

The Iterative Loop

I initially sketched solutions that stayed close to the original layout to avoid "re-learning" costs for existing users. Testing revealed that users felt motivated by the progress bars and specifically appreciated reading time estimates, as it helped them schedule their reading around their classes. However, the overall navigation still felt cluttered.

UX INSIGHT

Mental Model Conflict

While testing the original hero section, I noticed a consistent pattern of hesitation. Users didn't know if the highlighted book was "theirs" or an "advertisement."

The Confusion

"I see a book cover here, but I don't know if clicking it will take me to my last read or to a shop. I'm afraid to tap it."

The Solution

Replaced the "Featured" slot with a Personalized Dashboard that uses progress bars to signal ownership and status.

The Logic Shift

Old Flow: Browse back through the catalogue to find a book already in progress.

New Flow: One tap from the home screen (Resume Reading).

I wanted to put Statistics section at the top but it turned out to be too disruptive to the flow.

The Filter Problem

Before

Chaotic filters leading to 'Analysis Paralysis'.

After

Clean symmetry and truncated categories.

Chunking the Choices

Working memory fills up fast — the more filter options someone has to hold in mind at once, the more likely they are to hesitate or bail. So I grouped related filters under clear headings and truncated the rest behind Progressive Disclosure, only surfacing secondary information when requested. Chunking the choices let Maciek focus on one decision at a time instead of scanning everything at once.

The original navigation relied on a deep, nested hierarchy that forced students to navigate through multiple sub-menus just to resume a book. I restructured the Information Architecture to prioritize Recency and Accessibility. By flattening the navigation, I ensured that 'Active Reads' and 'Search' were always one tap away.

Accessibility Gap

Low color contrast and rigid text blocks of my initial redesign made the app difficult for users with visual impairments or dyslexia. Rather than patch it, I rebuilt the color palette and text hierarchy from scratch — this is reflected in the final interface below, not a leftover issue from the first pass.

05 / IMPACT

Impact & Conclusion

When I showed the redesign to the same group, the reaction was consistently positive — people described it as clearer and more intuitive than the original. I also revisited the Patron call-to-action specifically, since it was one of the clearest points of confusion: I rewrote the button copy to a direct "Dorzuć się" and moved it out of the primary hero position so it no longer competed with the core reading content.

Takeaway: Adhering to design principles is essential, but it is no substitute for deep research. Through this process, I learned that even the most 'correct' UI can fail if it doesn't align with the user's core goals. Sometimes the solution isn't a better button—it’s a new functionality altogether. UX research doesn't just polish an app; it defines the roadmap for what should be built next.

On AI: this project predates the current wave of AI design tooling. Coming back to it, the honest answer is that meeting today's workflow standards would mean folding AI into the process itself — synthesizing scattered research into themes faster, stress-testing more IA variants before committing to wireframes, and running a first heuristic pass ahead of user testing rather than after. The conclusions above wouldn't change; getting to them would just take less time.
