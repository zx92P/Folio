The Drive Behind The Project

A Failure of Official Standards

Back in 2022, with Poland heading to the World Cup in Qatar, I wanted to follow the national team and turned to the official platform. What I found was a design so broken it prompted 600+ reviews averaging 2 stars. A recent update at the time had deleted core navigation features, effectively locking fans out.

01 / THE PROBLEM

First Impressions vs. Reality

My intuition was immediate: the app felt stuck in the past - ; core navigation was buried behind a hidden side-drawer with nested menus, several taps from anything useful. To validate this, I audited recent updates and store feedback. The data was clear-the "digital home" of Polish football was causing massive user friction. This wasn't just a visual issue; it was a functional lockout.

"Who even let this out to use? Where’s the menu, where is the login which is required by the app? Nothing works here."
02 / RESEARCH FINDINGS

Research & Task Failure

I ran informal, semi-structured sessions with a small group of people I know who follow Polish football - adapting my questions as each conversation went rather than working from a fixed script. The task I kept coming back to: find a specific team and their detailed information.

I.

Lost in Navigation

Most people backed out to the home screen repeatedly while trying to reach a lineup, losing track of where they were.

II.

Menu Ambiguity

Core icons - menu, login - weren't recognized as menu or login at all on first glance.

III.

Slow to Basic Info

Getting something as simple as a lineup consistently took noticeably longer than it should.

IV.

No Perceived Affordance

The homepage's only prompt to check results is styled identically to the header and sponsor bar - same red, no button shape, no icon. I missed the text itself on first use, let alone read it as tappable.

03 / COMPETITIVE AUDIT

Learning from the Competition

To understand where the app's experience was failing at the time, I audited three leading sports competitors - ; FlashScore, Sport.pl LIVE, and SofaScore. While these platforms vary in scale, they share a common goal: delivering live match updates in seconds. Reviewing their App Store listings and mapping their core flows, I identified the industry "habits" that users have come to expect - and exactly where our app was breaking those promises.

Task: Check Live Score of "Team X"

Competitors
Open App → Tap "Live"
The App (2022)
Open App → Open Menu → Select League → Filter Live → Select Match

Strengths

  • Official brand authority
  • Exclusive behind-the-scenes content

Weaknesses

  • Core actions (menu, login, search) stripped from direct access, buried behind a hidden menu
  • Difficult & Unintuitive Navigation

Opportunities

  • Streamlined live-score tracking
  • Personalized "My Team" dashboards

Threats

  • User Churn due to Negative Reviews
  • Competitors Setting the UX Bar Higher

The SWOT analysis surfaced where the real opportunity was - it would have been easy to get lost fixing individual bugs, when the actual gap was that the app wasn't even meeting the baseline fans had come to expect elsewhere.

04 / USER PERSONA

The Exhausted Supporter

Understanding the environmental context of use was key. A fan like Tomek isn't browsing in a quiet room; he's often on a noisy job site or a moving train with limited time and one-handed mobile use.

Tomek, 35

Construction Tired Lifelong Fan

"I just want to see the score before my break ends. I don't want to visit five different websites."

Primary Need

A hierarchy that prioritizes My Schedule and Live Scores above promotional content.

To move beyond my own assumptions, I asked that same group to rank common app features from 1 (Critical) to 4 (Nice-to-Have). This data directly informed the new Information Architecture, ensuring the most vital data required the fewest taps.

Rank Feature User Intent
1 Match Schedule Planning viewership around work/life commitments.
2 Live Scores & Lineups Instant updates during match-day "burst" sessions.
3 News Post-match analysis, transfer news, and injury reports.
4 Multimedia Leisurely browsing of behind-the-scenes content.
05 / ITERATION

Learning from Failed Iterations

I used 'Crazy 8's' to ideate rapidly. My first designs actually failed-they created new issues. I dove back into wireframing and settled on a design that leveraged existing mobile habits.

The core issue wasn't just aesthetic; the app suffered from Information Architecture (IA) collapse. After the October 2022 update, users were left without a persistent navigation anchor, forcing fans to back out to the home screen repeatedly just to switch between news and scores.

The Legacy Logic (Broken)

A hidden side-drawer with nested menus. Users had to dig through several levels just to find basic match info.

PAIN POINT: BURIED IN NESTED MENUS

The New Logic (Streamlined)

A flat hierarchy using a persistent Bottom Tab Bar, consolidating 12 menu items into 4 logical buckets based on the priority ranking from research.

SOLUTION: 1 TAP TO CORE FEATURES
Why This Layout Won

Thumb-zone: the core menu sits in the bottom nav for one-handed use on a noisy job site or a moving train - Tomek's exact context.

Familiarity: standardized 'Home' and 'Search' icons meant no one had to learn a new pattern.

Reservation: most people I talked to agreed score accessibility was the priority, though a few felt the exclusive content was pushed too far out of view - a real trade-off, not a fully resolved one.

06 / IMPACT

Impact & Conclusion

When I showed the redesign to that same group and asked them to find a lineup again, the reaction was consistently positive. The vast majority completed the task almost immediately, without the repeated backtracking I'd seen in the original - testers who'd struggled for over a minute in the old flow found the lineup in seconds. It won't stand up as a lab-grade result, but the shift was not subtle.

Takeaway: Don't be scared to start over; there is no point in forcing a concept that is wrong at its core. Draw inspiration from established mobile habits to build familiarity and reduce user fatigue, but never stop validating.

On AI: this project predates the current wave of AI design tools. If I were tackling Tomek's problem today, I'd use AI in the process itself - ; synthesizing interview notes faster, testing more IA variants earlier, running a quick heuristic pass before user testing instead of after. It also points to a real feature idea: a smart notification that surfaces the one thing Tomek needs mid-shift - ; a score, a lineup change, a red card - ; instead of a feed he has to parse one-handed on a noisy job site. That's a proposal, not something I tested.
